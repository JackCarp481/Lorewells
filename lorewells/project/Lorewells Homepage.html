§ 05 — Why It Matters

A story kept is not a luxury.

For families, a finished film is the difference between remembering that a person existed and hearing them again — the voice, the timing, the truth of how they told it.

For communities, the archive is local history in first person: the wars, the ranches, the congregations, the ordinary work that built a place.

For the people themselves, being asked — seriously, at length, on the record — is a form of honor most never receive in their lifetime.

Children who know their family's stories grow up more resilient and more certain of who they are — the strongest single predictor of emotional wellbeing researchers have found.

— The "Do You Know?" Scale · Family Narrative Research, Emory University

§ 09 — The Founder

It began with one camera and one conviction.

Jacob founded Lorewells after years behind a camera taught him a simple thing: the most important footage he ever shot was never the most cinematic — it was the people who wouldn't be here next year, telling it their way.

Every film in the archive is made with the same patience: real light, real time, and the discipline to let a person finish their sentence.

Jacob · Founder & Director of Photography · Central Oregon, OR

§ 10 — The Apprenticeship

Preserve one generation. Train the next.

Every production carries an apprentice — a young filmmaker learning the craft of listening on camera. The stories stay; so does the skill to keep gathering them.

"When a person dies, we lose a library. We built Lorewells so we stop losing them."
